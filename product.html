What we actually do

We design and build the product, then stay until it runs.

A product fails far more often from scope than from code. Somebody lists twelve features, all twelve get half-built, and the thing ships as five tabs that each do a bit of something. Nobody opens it twice.

So the first week is spent deciding what the product is for, in one sentence, and what gets cut to protect that. Everything after runs against that sentence. If a feature cannot justify itself against it, it waits for version two.

Where speed matters more than completeness, that becomes a fast MVP. Where the product needs an operating layer around it, that is automation. Often it is both, in that order.

  • Custom productsBonus systems, tournament platforms, trackers, rooms. Built around the way your business already moves rather than around a template’s idea of it.
  • Agentic productsA product with the AI inside the workflow rather than bolted to the side. Mylo is the live example: the agent sits in the room with everyone.
  • Map and event appsLocation-first products where the map is the interface. See what is nearby, join it, and settle the money afterward.
  • Shared and verified spacesRooms, groups, and identity checks. Government ID matched to a selfie when the product needs people to trust who they are meeting.
